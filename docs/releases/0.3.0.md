# GoOS 0.3.0 (unreleased)

GoOS 0.3.0 is the ext4 release. It reads Linux ext4 partitions and, when
you ask for it, writes them through their JBD2 journal so that a crash of
the service or the machine leaves a volume GoOS, Linux and `e2fsck` all
recover. These notes grow with the release; the release check on the
tagged commit is the evidence they will cite.

## Scope

ext4 is read-write on additional volumes only: partitions of the boot disk
mounted below `/mnt` with `mount -o rw` or a configuration entry that ends
in `rw` (ADR 0226). The persistent volume, `/persist` with `/system`,
`/apps`, `/home` and `/state`, stays FAT32. 0.3.0 does not make ext4 the
system volume; that change needs its own review of the boot path, the
recovery fence, the installer and `disk-update`.

## ext4

- **Reading** (ADR 0220, 0221). A userspace parser checks features,
  checksums, group descriptors, extents and block maps, linear and hashed
  directories, sparse and unwritten ranges, and extended attributes, and
  refuses what it cannot interpret. Each mounted volume runs in its own
  `/bootstrap/ext4` process with its own block slice and file cache, in one
  of four slots beside the persistent volume (ADR 0222).
- **Links and metadata** (ADR 0223). The VFS follows symbolic links under
  the caller's root, at most 40 per lookup. Hard links, inode numbers,
  owners, modes and the four timestamps reach Linux programs through
  `stat`; GoOS grants nothing from them.
- **Writing** (ADR 0224, 0225, 0226). Allocation, extent trees, hashed
  directories, rename, replace, links and truncate commit through the JBD2
  journal in ordered-data mode, with Linux's orphan list for files removed
  while open. Each operation is durable when it returns. A read-write mount
  replays the journal first; `umount` leaves the volume clean for Linux.
- **Supported write profile.** 4 KiB blocks, an internal journal and the
  feature list in docs/userspace/ext4.md. Volumes with 1 or 2 KiB blocks,
  `uninit_bg` or block-mapped files mount read-only. Encryption, casefold,
  `inline_data`, `bigalloc`, `meta_bg`, external journals, fast commits and
  the orphan file are refused.

## Not in this release

- ext4 as the persistent or system volume.
- More than one disk, more than four additional volumes, automatic mounts
  of detected partitions.
- Writing extended attributes, owners or mode bits other than the
  read-only attribute.
- Sharing a mounted partition with Linux or another GoOS instance.
