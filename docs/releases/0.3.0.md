# GoOS 0.3.0

GoOS 0.3.0 is the ext4 release. It reads Linux ext4 partitions and, when
you ask for it, writes them through their JBD2 journal so that a crash of
the service or the machine leaves a volume GoOS, Linux and `e2fsck` all
recover.

## Scope

ext4 is read-write on additional volumes only: partitions of the boot disk
mounted below `/mnt` with `mount -o rw` or a configuration entry that ends
in `rw` (ADR 0226). The persistent volume, `/persist` with `/system`,
`/apps`, `/home` and `/state`, stays FAT32. 0.3.0 does not make ext4 the
system volume; that change needs its own review of the boot path, the
recovery fence, the installer and `disk-update`.

## ext4

- **Reading** (ADR 0220, 0221). A userspace parser checks features,
  checksums, group descriptors, extents and block maps, linear and hashed
  directories, sparse and unwritten ranges, and extended attributes, and
  refuses what it cannot interpret. Each mounted volume runs in its own
  `/bootstrap/ext4` process with its own block slice and file cache, in one
  of four slots beside the persistent volume (ADR 0222).
- **Links and metadata** (ADR 0223). The VFS follows symbolic links under
  the caller's root, at most 40 per lookup. Hard links, inode numbers,
  owners, modes and the four timestamps reach Linux programs through
  `stat`; GoOS grants nothing from them.
- **Writing** (ADR 0224, 0225, 0226). Allocation, extent trees, hashed
  directories, rename, replace, links and truncate commit through the JBD2
  journal in ordered-data mode, with Linux's orphan list for files removed
  while open. A read-write mount replays the journal first; `umount`
  leaves the volume clean for Linux.
- **Batched commits** (ADR 0227). Operations share journal transactions,
  as on Linux: a write is durable after `fsync`, the new shell builtin
  `sync`, `umount`, `reboot`, `poweroff`, or within one second. A create
  that cost two device flushes and 14 writes now costs about a hundredth
  of a flush; `fsync` still costs two flushes.
- **Indexed directories** (ADR 0229). A directory GoOS fills past one block
  becomes a hash tree, as Linux makes it, and `e2fsck` checks it clean at
  10000 names. Listing a hash-ordered directory no longer fails when the
  inodes it reads evict the leaf being listed.
- **Measured** (docs/development/ext4-validation.md). Under KVM a `stat`
  on ext4 takes 0.15 ms, a create 0.93 ms, `fsync` of 4 KiB 1.5 ms, and a
  64 MiB write 97 MiB/s; memory settles across repeated runs; a volume
  filled to `ENOSPC` refuses cleanly and takes writes again once freed.
  Linux 6.12 on the same node runs metadata calls 45 to 90 times faster
  and `fsync` five times faster; the table beside it shows each one. The
  VFS path cache and RAMFS name lookup got faster for every filesystem
  (ADR 0228).
- **Supported write profile.** 4 KiB blocks, an internal journal and the
  feature list in docs/userspace/ext4.md. Volumes with 1 or 2 KiB blocks,
  `uninit_bg` or block-mapped files mount read-only. Encryption, casefold,
  `inline_data`, `bigalloc`, `meta_bg`, external journals, fast commits and
  the orphan file are refused.

## Validation

`make release-check` runs the host gates `test-ext4-unit`,
`test-ext4-stress` and `test-ext4-mutations`, and the TCG boot gates
`test-volumes` and `test-ext4-rw`, beside the rest of the GoOS gates. The
mutation and read-write gates check what GoOS wrote with the pinned
`e2fsck -fn` and `debugfs` of e2fsprogs 1.47.2. On the Proxmox lab node,
`make test-pve-ext4` runs `fsbench` five times on a 1 GiB read-write
volume, fills one directory with 10000 names, walks a 100-directory chain
and fills the volume to `ENOSPC`; `tools/ext4fixture/linux-ext4-reference.sh`
runs the same `fsbench` on Linux 6.12 under the same KVM settings.
docs/development/ext4-validation.md lists each result, the commit it ran
on, and the numbers.

## Known limits

- One ext4 service serves the requests of one volume in turn, so 2, 4 or
  8 processes on one volume reach the throughput of one.
- Read-write mounts need 4 KiB blocks and the feature profile in
  docs/userspace/ext4.md; other volumes mount read-only or not at all.
- The hash-tree writer keeps one index level. A directory that needs more
  than 507 leaves refuses new names with `ENOSPC`.
- ext4 cannot be `/persist` in 0.3.0; the persistent volume stays FAT32.
- Do not hold one partition read-write in GoOS and in Linux or another
  GoOS instance at the same time.
- A power cut that tears the primary superblock leaves a volume GoOS
  recovers through the JBD2 journal. `e2fsck` 1.47.2 may refuse such a
  single-group image until you give it a backup superblock with `-b`.

## Not in this release

- ext4 as the persistent or system volume.
- More than one disk, more than four additional volumes, automatic mounts
  of detected partitions.
- Writing extended attributes, owners or mode bits other than the
  read-only attribute.
- Sharing a mounted partition with Linux or another GoOS instance.
